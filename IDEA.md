# PillCheck: idea

**Who:** adults in India who take several medicines from different doctors (diabetes, blood pressure, heart, thyroid,
pain, mood), and the family members who help them. Many read Tamil or Hindi more easily than English.

**Problem:** nobody looks at the whole list. Label warnings about combinations (warfarin with aspirin, clopidogrel with
omeprazole, levothyroxine with calcium) are in long English leaflets. People ask neighbours, or search, and get a guess.

**What changes:** the person names their medicines and gets, in their language, which pairs their official labels warn
about, with the label's own words quoted, and is sent to a pharmacist. Dose questions and emergencies go straight to a person.

**Measured by:** interaction pairs flagged, red-flag cases handed off, guesses about unknown medicines, dosing advice
and "safe together" claims, against a one-prompt baseline on the same cases (`ss measure`).
